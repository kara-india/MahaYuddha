# Market validation plan

This is a hypothesis-driven research plan, not a claim that market demand or sales have already been proven. Record sources, access dates, and confidence for every external finding before making investment decisions.

## Questions to answer
1. Which mobile action games compete for the same audience, session time, and spending?
2. Do players value a story-first progression path that leads into skill-based 1v1?
3. What visual fidelity is achievable on common Android devices within the budget?
4. Which Hindi/English story and voice presentation is most compelling?
5. Which monetization choices are accepted without undermining competitive fairness?
6. What licensing, trademark, cultural-consultation, and platform requirements apply?

## Competitor research matrix
For each comparable title, collect:
- Store rating and review themes, download/revenue estimates only when the source and methodology are explicit.
- Core loop, onboarding, session duration, PvP structure, progression, and live-ops cadence.
- Visual quality and performance across low/mid/high devices.
- Monetization: cosmetics, battle pass, ads, gacha, energy, pay-to-win complaints.
- Localization quality and regional marketing strategy.
- Evidence source, publication date, confidence, and unresolved gaps.

Use official store listings, developer announcements, public financial disclosures, reputable market-intelligence sources where available, and structured player-review sampling. Do not present estimates from third-party trackers as audited facts.

## Validation experiments
### A. Concept test
Show a concise pitch and visual references to 20–30 target players. Ask for unaided recall, strongest appeal, biggest concern, and willingness to try; avoid leading questions.

### B. Combat prototype test
Test movement, lock-on, dodge/parry, hit feedback, power activation, camera behavior, and rematch flow with a playable greybox before expensive art.

### C. Story and language test
Compare Hindi-first and English-first presentation, subtitles, voice-over expectations, and narrative clarity.

### D. Monetization trust test
Compare cosmetic-only purchases, season pass, and optional convenience. Explicitly test rejection of paid competitive power.

### E. Store-page test
Use a legally cleared title and original/authorized art. Measure qualified click-through and wishlist/pre-registration intent; do not buy installs until retention is measurable.

## Decision gates
- **Gate 1 — Concept:** players understand the differentiator and request a playable demo.
- **Gate 2 — Combat:** repeated playtests report responsive, legible combat and meaningful counterplay.
- **Gate 3 — Technical:** target Android devices sustain acceptable frame pacing; networking feasibility is demonstrated.
- **Gate 4 — Retention:** instrumented tests show users return and replay missions/duels.
- **Gate 5 — Scale:** only then evaluate ranked PvP expansion, 2v2, and survival modes.

Define numerical thresholds before each experiment, record sample size, and document negative results. Do not infer market-wide demand from a small convenience sample.
